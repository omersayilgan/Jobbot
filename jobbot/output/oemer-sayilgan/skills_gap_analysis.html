5. Eligibility and language — read before drawing conclusions

How much German these roles actually require

3 of 133 of these roles ask for fluent or C1-level German; you are at B1. On this evidence German is not what is gating your shortlist.

The honest caveat: that result partly reflects what this tool can see. Employers with public job-board APIs skew toward English-speaking companies; smaller and public-sector employers who do require the local language are largely invisible to it. So read this as “German is not gating the roles on your list”, not as “German does not matter in Munich”. Improving it still widens the market beyond what this document covers.

6. How this was measured

  • Document frequency, not word frequency. A posting naming Python nine times counts once. Raw word counts would have made recruiting boilerplate the top “skill” in this analysis.
  • Employer reach is tracked separately. Helsing writes a large share of the best-fit postings, so its house requirements would otherwise read as market-wide demand. Section 2 exists to separate the two.
  • Importance = 45% share of roles + 35% share of employers + 20% average fit of the roles asking. A skill demanded by your strongest matches outranks a commoner one demanded by weak matches.
  • Levels are judgements, not measurements. They were inferred from your CV and transcripts by setup.py, which distinguishes work evidence from coursework. Correct any of them in your profile's skills_taxonomy.yaml and re-run python3 run.py skills.
  • Limits. Only employers with a public job-board API are covered, and a skill absent from an advert is not always absent from the job.
